# Earth Analogs for Moon and Mars: the challenge

Official challenge page: https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/permanent-moon-base-locations-and-mars/

## The short version

**Find places on Earth that resemble the Moon or Mars closely enough that astronauts and engineers can practise there first, and explain how close the match really is.**

## The problem NASA describes

NASA plans a permanent base on the Moon (most likely near its south pole) and later trips to Mars. Before sending people and equipment, they test everything somewhere cheaper and safer: Earth.

They already do this at a handful of famous spots that look and behave a bit like other worlds:

- **Atacama Desert, Chile:** so dry it's like Mars.
- **Antarctica:** cold, empty and icy.
- **Iceland and Hawaii:** volcanic rock like the Moon's dark plains.
- **Haughton Crater, Canada, and Death Valley, USA.**

The trouble is that those few sites get used over and over, and nobody has searched the **whole planet** in an organised way for better or overlooked matches. **NASA wants teams to use open Earth, Moon and Mars data to find and describe new analog sites, and to be honest about where each one falls short.**

## A simple way to picture it

A **dating app for planets**. Give it a Moon or Mars location, and it shows you Earth places, each with a match score and a short explanation: "you both love cold and darkness, but you have air and it doesn't."

## What the tool should let you do

1. **Pick a target,** such as a likely Moon base site or a Mars landing zone.
2. **Describe it with real data:** how rough the ground is, how cold it gets, how much sunlight it gets, whether there's ice, dust storms or caves, and what the rock is made of.
3. **Search Earth for places that share some of those traits,** using NASA satellite data about Earth.
4. **Explain each match honestly:** what's similar, how similar, and where it breaks down. A spot might match the rock well but the cold badly.
5. **Say why it matters,** for example: "test a Moon rover's wheels here", "practise drilling for ice here", "look for signs of life in Mars-like conditions here".
6. **Show the results** as a map, an app, a ranking tool, a story or a written report. NASA leaves the format open.

## What makes a strong entry

- **Go beyond looks.** Two places can look alike but be very different underneath. Use data, not photos alone.
- **No place matches everything.** Being clear about the weak spots counts in your favour.
- **Find somewhere new.** NASA says not to just rediscover Antarctica or Atacama. Their words: "Surprise us."
- **Zoom in.** "The Sahara" is too broad. One crater, valley or lava tube is a stronger answer.
- **Think about processes, not only places.** Dust storms, ice movement and water slowly changing rock can be analogs too.
- **Know Earth's limits.** Earth can't fake low gravity, the Moon's lack of air, Mars's thin air, or space radiation. Saying so is part of good science.

## Things you can compare

- Rock, minerals and surface material
- Terrain: height, slopes, roughness, craters
- Temperature, sunlight and extremes
- Water, ice and frozen ground (resources a base could use)
- Dust, wind and erosion
- Life that survives extremes, and signs of past life
- Caves, lava tubes and other underground spaces
- How easy it is to land, drive, navigate or build there

## Data

All free. The full list NASA provides is in [GUIDELINES.md](../GUIDELINES.md#3-data).

## Who it's for

Mission planners and scientists choosing where to test gear, plus students and the public curious about why some corners of Earth feel like another planet.

## Competition

The challenge looks popular. A quick search on 2026-11-13 found at least eight public team repos for it, and most build the same thing: a web app that ranks Earth sites by similarity. Examples: [EarthTwin](https://github.com/es-ratt/earthtwin), [TwinWorlds](https://github.com/JimmyGuajan/twinworlds), [terrestrial-analog-finder](https://github.com/shishir-kuet/terrestrial-analog-finder). Standing out probably means a surprising location or a sharper, more honest way of explaining each match.
